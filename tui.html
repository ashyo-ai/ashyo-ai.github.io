TUI

Run ashyo with no arguments to open the interactive surface. It takes over the terminal's alternate screen, never relies on scrollback, and puts every decision in front of you before it happens.

Starting

$ ashyo
$ ashyo session resume 6f1c2a0e-3b7d-4c1e-9a2f-0d8e5b4c7a11

Without a session id, Ashyo creates a new session in the current directory. The terminal must be at least 24 columns by 12 rows; below that, input is held and a resize notice is shown. The mouse wheel scrolls whatever is under the pointer and a click moves focus.

Layout

Top to bottom: the transcript, an interaction pane that docks only when something needs you, a one-line status line, the composer, and the completion dock while you are typing a command or a path. The composer sits on the last row of the terminal with the status line directly above it; the dock drops out beneath the composer for as long as what you are typing has something to complete, lifting the composer by the rows it takes and putting it back on the bottom when it goes. The interaction pane takes at most 40% of the rows and the transcript always keeps at least five, so a long approval scrolls inside its box rather than pushing the conversation off screen. The composer is exactly as tall as what you have typed, up to five rows: it takes a row from the transcript for each line and gives it back as you delete. Past five rows it stops growing and scrolls to keep the line you are on in view. A line too long for the width counts as the rows it wraps to, the same as lines you ended yourself.

The interaction pane takes focus as it docks, so an approval never sits waiting for you to notice it. Whatever you were typing is left in the composer untouched, and when the pane closes focus goes back where it came from — the composer, or the transcript if that is what you were reading. A question with no options is the exception: it is answered by typing, so it leaves the composer focused.

Every line in the transcript is labeled with who produced it: you, the provider and model that answered, tool: <name> — <intent> for a call, the name of the tool and the line the model wrote saying what the call was for, ashyo for the harness's own reports, and advisor (note), advisor (concern), or advisor (blocker) for the advisor. A note the harness dropped is labeled — dropped: it is shown, and the model never read it. A concern or blocker that advisor.immuneTurns held back is labeled — held: it keeps its kind, interrupts nothing, and reaches the model with your next message. A call a blocker cut short says so where the call is, and a turn the advisor started opens with a line saying so. The transcript is a projection of the branch you are on, so a resume or a branch switch shows the same lines in the same places.

A compaction leaves a muted line where it happened, naming which of the three methods ran — ── compacted: shake ── — and nothing else; it is drawn the moment it happens, mid-turn included, and again when you reopen the session. A summary's line stands where the turns it covers were, with everything above it taken off the screen the way /clear takes turns off. Focus the transcript with Tab and press Enter on it to open it on the summary the model reads, and again to close it. The session tree is untouched by any of it: every turn is still a row, and Enter on a row above a summary gives you a branch with the whole conversation back.

Nothing drawn on the screen can drive the terminal. Every string Ashyo did not write itself — a model's answer, a tool's output, what you sent, an advisor line, a subagent's summary, the excerpts on the session tree and the session picker, and the tool, the intent, and the arguments of a call waiting on your approval — is stripped of terminal escape sequences and control characters before it is drawn. A fetched page that retitles your window, a command that repaints the screen, a result that moves the cursor: each is shown as the words it holds and nothing else. Colored command output is drawn uncolored, since a color is a sequence like any other; a tab becomes four spaces, so a row measures the width it takes; the line breaks are kept. The colors on the screen are Ashyo's own, applied after the strip, so nothing of your theme is touched.

The session log is untouched too: the strip is at the drawing, and the log keeps every byte the tool returned, because it is the record of what happened. The transcript search and the session tree's search read what the screen shows, so the bytes of a sequence are not searchable — a row is found by the words on it.

Scrolling

Everything that scrolls scrolls the same way and shows the same scrollbar: the transcript, the interaction pane, the session tree, the session picker, the session box, the key help, each of the settings screen's lists, the completion dock, the history dock, and the model picker. The bar is one column on the pane's right edge — an accent-colored thumb on a muted track, the thumb as long a share of the bar as what is on screen is of the whole, never shorter than a cell — and it is drawn only while there is more than fits. The column is kept for it either way, so text wraps to the same width whether or not a bar is showing: the transcript changes height with every composer line and every dock, and a conversation near one screen tall is not re-wrapped each time it crosses the edge.

The mouse wheel moves whatever is under the pointer, three rows a tick. PageUp and PageDown move by one window. ↑ and ↓ move a row in a pane and a highlight in a list. Whether scrolling brings the highlight along is each list's own rule: the dock, the session tree, and the picker bring theirs, so Tab and Enter never act on a row that has scrolled out of sight, while the settings screen's lists leave theirs where it is, so scrolling the theme list previews nothing. The highlight comes back on screen with the next ↑ or ↓.

The composer is the exception. It is an editor rather than a list: past five rows it scrolls itself to keep the line you are on in view, and it draws no bar.

Keys

Enter
Send the composer. With the completion dock showing it completes the highlighted row and closes the dock instead, and the press after that sends.
Shift-Enter / Alt-Enter / Ctrl-J
Insert a newline, in the composer and in the argument editor. All three work in every terminal. The one thing a terminal can take away is Shift-Enter itself: telling it apart from Enter needs the keyboard enhancements Ashyo asks for at startup, and a terminal that does not grant them — tmux among them — sends a plain Enter for the chord unless you have bound it to Esc Enter, which arrives as Alt-Enter and writes a newline. F1 says which terminal you are in. None of the three answers the interaction pane: that takes Ctrl-Y or Ctrl-N, so a chord still in flight from a half-typed message can never allow a tool call.
↑ / ↓ (empty composer)
Walk this project's input history: ↑ brings back the last thing you sent, ↑ again the one before it, ↓ steps back towards the newest, and one ↓ past it leaves the composer empty again. With anything typed in the composer they are the editor's own keys and move the cursor between its lines, and with the completion dock showing they move its highlight.
Tab / Shift-Tab
Cycle focus: transcript, interaction pane, composer.
PageUp / PageDown
Scroll by a page: the settings list holding the highlight, an open overlay, the completion dock, the history dock, or the model picker while the composer has focus, otherwise the focused pane. An approval takes focus as it docks, so with it and the dock both open these keys page the approval. See Scrolling.
Ctrl-Up / Ctrl-Down
Scroll the focused pane by a line.
g / G
Jump to the top or bottom of the transcript.
Enter (transcript focused)
Open or close a compaction summary's marker on the summary the model reads. With no summary on the pane it does nothing.
/
First in the message, open the completion dock on the commands; starting any later word, open it on the entries of the directory the word names. With the transcript focused, search it; n and N step through matches.
Ctrl-T
Step the reasoning effort up through the active model's levels, wrapping. See Reasoning effort.
Ctrl-S
Open the session picker.
Ctrl-R
Open the history dock on everything this project has sent, newest first with each entry's age. Only with the composer focused, and not while you are editing a tool call's arguments; an overlay owns the key for as long as it is up.
← / → (session tree open)
Fold the open turn back to its question, or the highlighted branch away; open the branch again, and then the turn. See Session tree.
Ctrl-L (session tree open)
Label the highlighted row, so the branch is found again by name — the question itself on a folded turn. Enter saves it, Esc cancels. See Session tree.
Ctrl-F (session tree open)
Fork the branch down to the highlighted row into a session of its own and carry on in it — down to the end of the turn on a folded one. The original is left where it was. See Session tree.
F1
Toggle key help. /keys does the same.
← / →
Move the highlighted option in the interaction pane.
Ctrl-Y (interaction focused)
Answer the interaction pane with the highlighted option. Enter, Ctrl-Enter, the digit keys, and all three newline chords do nothing there, because the pane takes focus the moment it docks and a key already on its way to the composer must not be able to allow a tool call. Ctrl-Y works in every terminal and inside tmux, with nothing to turn on.
Ctrl-N (approval focused)
Deny the approval, whichever option is highlighted — you do not have to move to Deny first. A question or a plan review has no "no" to give, so there Ctrl-N does nothing at all rather than guessing which of the author's options means refusal. Like Ctrl-Y, it works everywhere.
e
Edit a tool call's arguments before allowing it.
Esc
Dismiss the completion dock, clear the session tree's search or close an overlay, close the search; otherwise interrupt the running turn, or cancel a pending login. An Esc that dismissed the dock has acted: it interrupts nothing and starts no Esc Esc pair.
Esc Esc
Open the session tree, when the first press had nothing to close, interrupt, or cancel. Two presses within half a second.
Ctrl-C Ctrl-C
Quit. Two presses within 500 ms, the same window Esc Esc uses; a single Ctrl-C does nothing at all — it stops no turn, quits nothing, and writes nothing. With no turn running Ashyo leaves at once and the session records a normal exit. With a turn running it asks first: see Quitting. /quit does the same.

Quitting

Quitting an idle conversation needs no confirmation — nothing is lost — so Ctrl-C Ctrl-C and /quit both leave at once, and the session's last line records a normal exit. Quitting while a turn is running throws that turn away, so it asks first.

The question docks where approvals dock, as A turn is running. Quit anyway? [y/N], and takes focus as it opens. The turn keeps running while it waits: the answer keeps streaming into the transcript behind it, and a turn that finishes while the question is up does not dismiss it.

y
Quit. A turn still running is interrupted and the session records an interrupt exit; if it finished while the question was up there was nothing to interrupt, and the exit is a normal one.
n / Esc
Dismiss the question. The turn keeps running, nothing is written, and focus goes back to the pane it came from — including an approval the question was covering, which is untouched and still waiting for you.
everything else
Nothing. Enter above all, because Enter is the key already on its way to the composer when a question appears under it. The question owns every key while it is up, so Tab, F1, and a further Ctrl-C do nothing either.

Either way, leaving is a clean exit: Ashyo settles the turn, writes the session's exit line, drains memory consolidation, and exits 0.

Slash commands

A message beginning with / is a command. It is never sent to the model and never becomes a turn in the log.

CommandEffect
/model [<provider>/<model>]Switch this conversation to one model with no fallback. Recorded in the log. Nothing is written to the transcript; the status line's model field carries the switch at once. Refused while a turn is running. With nothing after it, it opens the model picker instead — the same switch, chosen from a list rather than typed.
/thinking <level>Set the reasoning effort to one of the active model's levels. Recorded, survives later model switches, refused mid-turn. Nothing is written to the transcript; the status line's effort field carries the level at once. A level the model does not offer is refused with the ones it does. With nothing after it, it asks for a level. See Reasoning effort.
/rename <title>Rename the session. Nothing is written to the transcript; the status line's title field and the picker carry the new name at once. With nothing after it, it asks for a title.
/planToggle plan mode. Nothing is written to the transcript; the status line's mode and plan fields carry the switch. See below.
/plan <request>Enter plan mode and send the request as the next turn.
/clearClear the model's context. The session, its title, its model, its reasoning effort, its mode, and every turn in it are kept; only what the next request carries is emptied. The transcript empties with it and says nothing; the turns are still in the session, above the clear node in the tree. Recorded in the log as a node in the tree. Refused while a turn is running, and refused when nothing has been recorded since the last clear. See Sessions.
/cloneCopy this conversation and carry on in the copy. The log, the scratch directory, and the session of every subagent the conversation ran are all copied under new ids; the copy opens on the branch the original was on, under the same title, model, reasoning effort, mode, and plan. Nothing is written to the transcript; the status line's session field carries the new id and the pane is unchanged. The original is left exactly as it was and stays in the picker, resumable. Refused while a turn is running, and while a subagent is still running; a live advisor is waited out instead, and what it had said and the model had not yet read carries into the copy. See Sessions.
/forkCopy the branch you are on and carry on in the copy. The path from the start of the conversation to the leaf you are on, the sessions of the subagents that branch ran, and the scratch directory are copied under new ids; what the other branches hold stays with the original. The copy opens on its leaf, under the title, model, reasoning effort, mode, and rules that branch carried. Nothing is written to the transcript; the status line's session field carries the new id and the pane is unchanged. The original is left exactly as it was, every branch intact, and stays in the picker, resumable. Refused while a turn is running, and while a subagent is still running; a live advisor is waited out instead, and what it had said about the forked path carries into the copy. See Sessions.
/sessionOpen the session box: this session's id, the provider your next turn would run on, what the branch you are on holds, and what it has cost. Nothing is written to the transcript and nothing is recorded. Not refused while a turn is running.
/login openrouter|openai-codex [--device]Log in without leaving the session. Esc cancels.
/logout openrouter|openai-codexRemove a credential.
/keysOpen the key help.
/treeOpen the session tree. Esc Esc does the same.
/resumeOpen the session picker. Ctrl-S does the same.
/settingsOpen the settings screen. Theme is the one row wired to a setting; the rest are placeholders, and every other setting lives in the config files.
/quitQuit. Asks first while a turn is running; Ctrl-C Ctrl-C does the same. See Quitting.

Completion

One dock, two lists: the slash commands, when your message begins with a slash, and the entries of a directory, when a later word does. Both are listed, narrowed, scrolled, and completed the same way, and the dock's border says which of them you are looking at.

The dock follows what the composer holds. Whenever the first character of your message is a / — leading spaces and blank lines aside, exactly as Ashyo decides a message is a command — and something matches what you have typed so far, a dock is open directly beneath the composer, listing every command above alphabetically by name — clear, clone, fork, keys, login, logout, model, and so on to tree, with no slash and no argument after it — beside what it does. Three of those lines say what is set rather than what the command does, and follow the session as it moves: model reads Model: openai-codex/gpt-5.6-luna, thinking reads Thinking: xhigh — Thinking: none on a model with no reasoning control — and plan reads Plan: off, Plan: on while planning, or Plan: on (plan.md) once a plan-mode turn has written a plan and the review box has offered it. The F1 key help lists the same lines, values included, and builds them when you open it, so the row you read there and the row you read under the composer always agree. The dock is the list you scan for a name while typing, so it is the one that is sorted; the F1 key help keeps the order the commands are declared in, which is the order it reads in. The table above and the F1 key help are where each command's argument is spelled out. Each further character keeps the commands whose name starts with what you have typed, in the same order, so /t leaves thinking and tree. Every edit asks the question again, so nothing has to be reopened: /xy matches nothing and shows no dock, and the two Backspacees back to / bring the whole list straight back. A pasted /mo opens it on model the same way, and so does a slash typed after a stray space or on the second line of a message you started with Shift-Enter. A slash with a character in front of it — src/, and/or, 1/2 — is an ordinary character, a slash starting a later word is a path, and a slash with the transcript focused still starts the transcript search.

↑ / ↓
Move the highlight, scrolling the list when it reaches the edge. The highlighted row is drawn in the accent color throughout — its ❯ marker, its name, and its description — so the row you are about to act on reads as one thing; on a terminal that drops the color the marker still says which row it is.
mouse wheel
Scroll the dock, three rows a tick, bringing the highlight with it so Tab and Enter always take a row you can see. The transcript stays where you left it.
PageUp / PageDown
Scroll the dock by one window of rows, the highlight following the same way. Like every key in this list, these reach the dock only while the composer has focus: an approval docked above it owns them for as long as it is up.
Tab
Complete the highlighted row into the composer, and leave the dock following the text — a completed command name takes it on to that command's arguments, and a completed directory into that directory.
Enter
Complete the highlighted row into the composer exactly as Tab does, then close the dock, the same dismissal Esc gives: it is back on your next edit. Nothing is sent by it. What you are left with is the completed text with no dock under it, and the Enter after that is the composer's and sends. So a command that takes no argument costs two presses, one to complete and one to send, and so does a command whose name you have already typed in full: a row the text already spells still completes and closes. A message is only ever sent by a key that reaches the composer with no dock showing.
Esc
Dismiss the dock and nothing else. What you typed stays in the composer, and the transcript has those rows back until your next edit — the next character, Backspace, or paste derives the dock again and shows it if anything matches. Nothing else brings it back before then: not a model switch that changes the candidates, not a resize, not an approval docking above the composer.

Once a command's name is complete and followed by a space — typed, or added by Tab — the dock lists that command's arguments, drawn from the session as it stands: the levels the active model offers for /thinking, the provider names for /login and /logout with --device offered after a provider, and the active role's configured models for /model. The names are listed alphabetically — the providers, and the models by their full provider/model reference — while the levels keep the model's own scale, lowest to highest, because that is the order you read to find the next level up rather than a list of names you scan. Typing narrows them by what appears anywhere in the candidate, so h leaves high and xhigh. /rename and /plan take free text nothing can offer, so the dock goes on the space after their name and the shape of the argument is drawn after the cursor instead, in the muted gray: /rename <title>, /plan <request>. It is the same text the table above spells the argument with, and it is drawn rather than typed — the composer still holds /rename and its space, so Backspace takes the space and brings the command list back, the first character you type lands where the hint was and takes it off screen, and Enter sends the bare command with nothing in place of the hint — /rename with no title asks for one, /plan with no request toggles plan mode as it always has. Move the cursor back into the name and the hint goes until the cursor is at the end again. A prefix nothing matches closes the dock the same way, leaving what you typed in the composer and coming back the moment an edit leaves something matching again.

Paths

A word that begins with / anywhere but the start of your message is a path, and the dock lists what is in it: type see /ho and home/ is under the composer. The word is split at its last slash — everything up to and including it is the directory, the rest is what you are matching — and the dock lists that directory's entries whose names start with it, alphabetically, with a / on every directory. The border names the directory being listed. An entry beginning with a dot is listed only once you have typed the dot. A directory that is not there, or that cannot be read, lists nothing and shows no dock, the same as a command name nothing matches. Nothing is remembered between keys: every keystroke reads the directory again, so a file written while you are typing is on the list at the next one.

Tab replaces what you have typed with the highlighted entry. A directory arrives with its slash, so the dock moves into it and you keep going: see /ho, Tab, mf, Tab, co, Tab leaves see /home/mfiano/code/ with that directory listed. A file arrives as it is, with nothing left to complete, and the dock goes. Enter completes the same way and closes the dock; the Enter after that sends the message. Only what is before the cursor is replaced — the rest of the line and every other word stay where they are — so a path completed in the middle of a sentence leaves the sentence around it alone.

The first word of a message is always the command list's: /home typed at the start lists commands, or nothing, never paths. A path in a command's free-text argument is completed, though — /rename /home/x — because the command is offered its own candidates while it has any and the path's once it has none. Only absolute paths open anything: src/, ./x, ../x, and ~/x are ordinary text.

The dock shows tui.commandCompletionLimit rows at a time — ten by default — and scrolls the rest behind the scrollbar every scrolling pane shows, on its right edge, saying where you are in the list. A short terminal shows fewer: the dock takes what is left after the status line, a docked interaction pane, the composer, and the transcript's five-row minimum, and a terminal with nothing left to give opens no dock at all. The dock and a docked approval can be open at once, one below the composer and one above it; closing either leaves the other where it is.

Input history

Everything you send from the composer is remembered: every message, and every command, including the ones Ashyo answers itself. With the composer empty, ↑ brings back the newest, ↑ again the one before it, and so on to the oldest, where a further ↑ stays put rather than wrapping around. ↓ walks back towards the newest and one ↓ past it leaves the composer empty, exactly as it was before you started. An entry comes back whole, line breaks and all, so a message written over four lines is four lines in the composer again, ready to edit or send.

The walk continues for as long as the composer still holds what was recalled into it. The first character you type — or delete, or paste — ends it: the text is yours from then on, ↑ and ↓ go back to moving the cursor between its lines, and nothing you have typed is ever taken away by a keypress. Sending starts the next walk from the top. A composer you have typed into recalls nothing at all, and neither does the composer while you are editing a tool call's arguments. The completion dock never competes for the two keys: it only opens over a message being typed, which is never the empty composer a recall starts from, and a recalled command leaves it closed until your next edit.

The history belongs to the project rather than to the session: it is the directory the session records — the same one the session picker groups by, or the directory Ashyo is running in for a session that records none — so opening a session from another repository brings that repository's history with it. It is kept in one file per project under $XDG_DATA_HOME/ashyo/history/, so it survives restarts and is shared by every Ashyo running in that project: each appends what it sends, and each reads the file again when it needs the list, so what the other window sent is there on your next ↑. Sending something already in the history moves it to the top rather than adding a copy, so a command you run all day sits once, at the top. The newest tui.inputHistoryLimit entries are kept — a thousand by default — and older ones are dropped as new ones arrive. A history that cannot be written says so once on a harness line: your messages are sent as usual, and nothing is recalled.

History dock

A thousand entries are more than ↑ reaches one at a time, so Ctrl-R with the composer focused opens them as a list. It docks under the composer, where the completion dock docks, with History on its border: this project's entries, newest first, ten rows at a time with the scrollbar every scrolling pane shows beside the rest. Ten is fixed and is not tui.commandCompletionLimit — that limit sizes a list you type a name against, and this one is a list you read. A short terminal shows fewer, taking what is left after the status line, a docked pane, the composer, and the transcript's five-row minimum, and one with nothing left to give opens no dock at all. Opening it reads the project's file again, so what another Ashyo in the same project sent is on the list a moment later.

Each row is its entry on one line: a message written over four lines is drawn with a space where each break was, and anything too long for the row is cut with …. The last cells of every row are its age, in the muted gray, in one column down the right edge: now for anything under ten seconds old, then 42s, 3m, 7h, 14d, 2y — one unit, always the coarsest that still says something, because the column is read to tell this morning's command from last month's rather than to measure it.

Whatever is in the composer is the filter, and an empty composer lists everything. Typing narrows the list to the entries holding what you have typed, wherever it appears in them and whatever its case, and Backspace widens it again. The completion dock does not derive while the history is up, so a / typed here narrows the history to the entries with a slash in them rather than listing the commands. Typing past the last match closes the dock the same way a command prefix nothing matches does, leaving every character in the composer; Ctrl-R opens it again on what is left.

Model picker

/model with nothing after it opens a picker under the composer, where the completion dock opens, with models on its border. It lists every model Ashyo knows about with what each one costs, how much it holds, and — measured here, from your own turns — how fast it answers. Enter on a row switches this branch to that model, exactly as typing the reference out does.

The rows are two groups. At the top, the models most recently chosen with /model in any session, newest first, at most five and one row per model however often you picked it — the handful you actually move between. Then a rule, and below it every other model Ashyo knows, sorted by reference. The model your next turn would run on ends its reference with •, and the highlight opens on the first row.

Each row is the provider/model reference on the left and four columns on the right, in the muted gray, lined up down the edge of the whole list:

openai-codex/gpt-5.3-codex                3.7s  23t/s  272k       $4/20
openrouter/z-ai/glm-4.6                                205k  $0.43/1.75
openrouter/nex-agi/nex-n2.5-mini:free                  262k        free
ColumnWhat it is
3.7sTime to the provider's first output, averaged over your own turns on that model. Blank until you have run one.
23t/sOutput tokens a second, over the same turns. Blank until you have run one.
272kThe context window, as the provider publishes it.
$4/20Dollars per million tokens, in and out. A model that costs nothing reads free; a model nobody publishes a price for is blank.

How many of the four are drawn depends on the width of your terminal. Below 76 columns there is room for what a model holds and what it costs, and no more. From 76 the throughput joins them, and from 96 the time to first token as well.

The last row of the dock describes the highlighted model in words: the catalog's name for it, then each column spelled out, then what it can be asked to do — GPT 5.3 Codex · 3.7s to first token · 23 t/s · 272k context · 128k output · $4/20 per M · reasoning · vision. A reference no provider list names reads no catalog entry: it is still a model you can switch to, and it is still a row.

Searching and choosing

The composer is the search, as it is for the history dock. An empty composer lists everything; each character you type narrows both groups to the models whose reference or catalog name holds what you typed, wherever it appears and whatever its case, and Backspace widens them again. A group left with nothing goes, and the rule goes with it. The completion dock does not derive while the picker is up, so a / typed here narrows models rather than listing commands.

↑ / ↓
Move the highlight, stepping over the rule between the groups.
mouse wheel, PageUp / PageDown
Scroll the list, bringing the highlight with it so Enter always takes a row you can see.
Enter
Switch this branch to the highlighted model, close the dock, and empty the composer. A model your config already lists keeps that entry's reasoning effort. The status line's model field carries the switch; nothing is written to the transcript, and nothing is sent. Your next /model lists it first.
Esc
Close the dock. Nothing is recorded and the composer is exactly as you left it.
Tab
Nothing. Everywhere else Tab writes the highlighted row into the composer, and a model is not text to write.

The picker shows tui.commandCompletionLimit rows at a time — ten by default — with the description row below them and the scrollbar beside the rest. A short terminal shows fewer, and one with nothing left to give opens nothing. /model while a turn is running opens nothing either: it is refused with the same line the switch gives, because the dock is there to make a switch.

Where the rows come from

Three lists, joined:

  • OpenRouter's public model list. No key needed. It gives each model's name, context window, largest output, price, and whether it takes a reasoning effort or an image.
  • Codex's own model list, read with the openai-codex login you already have. It gives each model's slug, display name, context window, and whether it takes an image, and leaves out the models Codex hides from its own picker. Without a Codex login, or when the fetch fails, Ashyo's own shipped list of Codex models stands in; a fetch that succeeds replaces it. Codex sells a subscription rather than tokens, so a Codex row's price is what OpenRouter charges for the same OpenAI model, and the description row says so.
  • Every model reference your config names, for any role. A model you have written down is always a row, whether or not either provider has heard of it.

The two fetched lists are kept in $XDG_CACHE_HOME/ashyo/models.json, default ~/.cache/ashyo/models.json. Ashyo refreshes them in the background: at startup when a piece is missing or more than a day old, and once a day while it runs, each list on its own so one failing leaves the other fresh. Nothing waits on the network — the picker opens at once on what is already known and gains the rest when the fetches land — and nothing about a refresh reaches the transcript: a failure leaves that list as it was and writes one line to Ashyo's log file. Deleting the file costs one fetch and nothing else. ashyo -p fetches nothing at all.

The two speed columns come from your own turns and from nowhere else. Every answer records how long its provider took to say anything and how long it took to finish, in the session log beside the token counts, so the numbers survive restarts and are read back across every session you have. A model's throughput is its output tokens over its time across its most recent 256 answers; its time to first token is the average over those of them that streamed before they finished. A model you have never run has both columns blank, and an answer that arrived in one piece counts toward the throughput and not toward the latency.

Reasoning effort

Reasoning effort is model-specific. Every model offers an ordered set of levels drawn from none, minimal, low, medium, high, xhigh, max: Ashyo ships the sets for the models it knows, a model it does not know offers low, medium, high, and a config entry can declare or replace a set with reasoningEfforts.

Ctrl-T steps the effort one level up the active model's set and wraps past the strongest back to the weakest, recording and refusing exactly as /thinking does. It starts from the level in effect, and from nothing at all it takes the weakest level above none. On a model with no reasoning control it says so and records nothing.

/thinking accepts only a level the active model offers, and what it records is that choice. What a request carries is the choice mapped into the set of whichever model runs: the nearest level that model offers, and on a tie the weaker one, so a mapping never spends more than you asked for. The same mapping applies to a fallback model that takes a turn after the first one fails.

So a chosen xhigh on a model whose levels stop at high sends high, and the status line's effort field reads high with effortChosen reading xhigh beside it; switching back to a model that offers xhigh sends xhigh again, with nothing new recorded. A model declared with no reasoning control is sent no effort at all, draws no effort field, and answers /thinking <level> by saying so.

Approvals

When a tool call matches an ask rule, or no rule at all, the interaction pane docks with the tool name, what the model said the call was for, the full arguments, the rule that matched, the mode and profile, and anything your before-hooks said about the call. Nothing is truncated; the pane scrolls.

There are two answers, Allow and Deny. Each covers the one call in front of you, and neither writes a rule. If you want the decision to stick, put it in the config file; it applies on the next call without a restart.

The pane takes focus as it docks and opens with Deny highlighted. ← and → move the highlight, Ctrl-Y confirms it, and Ctrl-N denies outright whichever option is highlighted. Nothing else answers. Enter, Ctrl-Enter, the digit keys, and every newline chord — Shift-Enter, Alt-Enter, Ctrl-J — do nothing at all: the keys you were about to press to write or send a message must never be the keys that allow a call you have not read, and focus moves to the pane the moment it arrives. Ctrl-Y and Ctrl-N are the two chords none of that typing produces, and unlike the Enter chords they need nothing from your terminal: they arrive intact from every terminal and through tmux, with no option to turn on.

Press e to load the arguments into the composer as JSON. Enter sends the edited call as an Allow; Esc returns to the prompt. JSON that does not parse is refused in place and your text is kept. The log records the call the model intended, the call you approved, and the call that ran, as three separate fields. An edit to an agent call may narrow the child's access but never widen it.

Plan mode

/plan switches the current conversation, on the current branch, to the plan role and a read-only profile: shell is unavailable, and edit and write work only beneath the session's scratch directory. The model is told to investigate without changing the working tree and to write a decision-complete plan to plan.md in that scratch directory. The status line's plan field shows the path and whether the file exists yet.

When a turn ends and plan.md is non-empty, a review docks with three choices: approve and execute, refine, and stop planning. It takes focus and opens on the first of them, and Ctrl-Y answers it; Ctrl-N does nothing here, since a review has no Deny. Approving records the plan and its SHA-256 in the log as an immutable event, switches back to the normal profile, and sends the plan as the next user turn with the instruction to execute it exactly as written. Nothing forks: the plan and its execution are one branch.

Plan mode needs a configured plan role and cannot be toggled while a turn is running.

Overlays

Session tree

/tree, or two Esc presses with nothing to close or interrupt, draws the branch structure of the current session, roots first. Every row opens with one mark and then reads what the node is, a colon, and what it holds: ▶ user: It's done., • assistant: Root cause confirmed from crash.txt…, • tool: shell — read the crash log. The mark is ▶ or ▼ where there is a turn to open or close, and • on every other row. A message is labelled by who said it and shows its text on one line, with a space where each break was and anything too long cut with … at the box's edge; a tool call shows the name of the tool and, after it, what the call was for in the model's own words, or the name alone for a call that said nothing; every other kind is named and shows nothing — model, thinking, mode, title, todo, clear, compaction, trim, hook, decision, rule, approved plan, exit — and the row the whole session hangs from says session. One kind of node is never a row at all: the message a tool's output comes back in, which is from you but has no text in it — there is one for every call the session makes, and they were a third of the rows of a long session. What came after such a message hangs from the call above it, the way a row any narrowing hides already does.

The box is a list of turns. A turn is a question — a message you sent that has text in it — and everything below it on its branch until the next question, a clear, a fork, or the end: the answers to it, the calls they made, and what those read. Every turn opens folded to its question, so /tree on a long session is the questions you asked, one row each, however many answers and calls sit under them. → opens the turn under the highlight, and its rows are drawn in the question's own column, since a turn is one thing and not a branch off it. ← on any row of an open turn folds it back and takes the highlight to the question. A folded turn reads ▶, an open one ▼, and every row that begins no turn — the session header, a clear, an answer, a tool call, a node right below a fork, a question with nothing under it to open — reads • in that same column. One column, one mark, on every row: ▶ user: … and • assistant: … start in the same place, and there is nothing after the mark but what the row says.

A row is a message, a tool call, a clear, a compaction, a trim, or the session header. The other records a session keeps beside them — a model, thinking level, or mode you changed, a rename, the model's todo list, a permission decision or rule, a hook that ran, a subagent's start and result, the advisor's own records, and the exit written when you left — are on the branch and in no view: a row of one would say nothing but its kind, and switching to it would continue from the same conversation as the row above it. A folded turn still ends where the conversation did: Enter on it lands past the records hidden after its last row, so a model you changed right after an answer is the model that branch carries on with.

A folded turn acts at its end: Enter on one switches to the last node of the turn and Ctrl-F forks there, because the end is where the conversation carried on from — to switch to a point inside a turn, open it first. Ctrl-L is the one key that stays on the question, since what it names is the row you are reading. A question that never got an answer — one you interrupted and sent again — is a bare row with nothing to open.

How far in a row sits is how many branches you abandoned to get to it, and nothing else. A node with one child sits in its parent's column, so a conversation that never branched runs straight down the left edge however long it is: a mark under a mark is its child, and there are no lines at all. Where a node has several children, one of them keeps the column and every other steps in one column. The one that keeps it is the child whose branch the conversation is on — the branch holding the row the box opens on — and at a fork off that branch, the newest child. The branches that stepped in are drawn first, in the order you made them, and the column carries on below them, so the next turn of the conversation you are on is always the next row of the same column and never below a branch of unknown length.

A branch that stepped in hangs from a ├─ on its first row and carries a │ down the rest of it, with a │ in every column further left whose own conversation resumes below, so the line you are on is seen passing the branch by. The lines run into the marks: a ├─ reaches the first mark of the branch it opens, and a │ runs down into the mark of the row that carries its column on, so no line ends above a blank. └─ is never drawn: a column always carries on under the branches that stepped out of it. One prompt interrupted and asked again, with the first two turns opened and the last still folded, reads:

▼ user: Why is the importer crashing on startup?
• assistant: One hit: the old `~` project's d…
├─ ▼ user: That moves to the sys project, and th…
│  • assistant: I moved it and …
▶ user: That moves to the claude project, and…

The mark and the label are drawn in the user color on what you said and the assistant color on what the model said; every row nobody spoke is muted, and so are the │ and ├─ lines a stepped-in branch carries, which are structure rather than something anybody said. The mark carries that color whichever glyph it is: the ▶ on a question of yours is in the user color, exactly as its • would be. Nothing marks the node the conversation is on: the tree opens with the highlight on the row holding it, which is the question of its turn while that turn is folded. The highlighted row is led by a ❯ in the accent color, in a column of its own at the box's left edge before the lines, and the whole row sits on a band of selectedBg — a step lighter than a dark terminal, a step darker than a light one — which leaves the row's own colors on it: the mark and label in the color of whoever spoke, the lines muted, and what was said in the terminal's own color. A row that cannot be switched to says (not selectable) — the session header, which is the empty root above the first turn, and anything below a node the session holds no event for. Enter switches; the transcript re-projects onto the chosen path. Send a message from an earlier node and you have a new branch; the old one is untouched and both survive a restart. See Sessions.

Tab cycles the box through four views, starting from the one it opens in: all, every row there is; assistant-only, the model's answers; no tools, everything but the tool calls; and labels-only, the rows you have labelled. The title names the view you are looking at. What a view decides is which rows an open turn has, not which turns there are: your questions are rows in the first three whatever else those views hide, so assistant-only is the questions with their answers under them and the calls gone. There is no user-only view any more, because a box of folded turns is that view and it is the one /tree opens on.

A node a view hides takes none of its descendants with it — a row whose parent is hidden hangs from the nearest row still shown — so a turn is never lost because something above it in the branch was. The forks are read off the rows you can see: a view that has taken every other child of a fork away leaves the one child still shown in its parent's column, so what is on screen never steps in for a branch that is not on it. labels-only is the one view that also keeps the rows above the ones it is for, and the one where a question you never labelled is not a row: it is the view for finding what you named, so it shows those rows with the rows they hang from, and it opens the turn holding a label on that label.

Typing narrows the rows: every letter is added to a search, and the rows left are the ones whose content says it, with the path down to each kept so every hit is read where it sits in the session. What is searched is what the row shows — a message's text, a tool call's name and what it was for — and the label you wrote on it, so a branch you saved under a name is found by typing that name, even on a row that shows nothing of its own. A record that is never a row is never searched, whatever label was written on it while it still had one. Never the user, assistant, or tool label beside it, so typing user finds the messages that talk about users rather than every message you sent. Case is ignored. Backspace takes a letter back. The view and the search compose: what is on screen is the rows that pass both, and the title carries the text you have typed. A narrowing that hides the highlighted row moves the highlight to the first row you can switch to, the way the completion dock's does. Esc clears the text when there is any and closes the box when there is none; the box opens on all with nothing typed and every turn folded every time, whatever the last visit left behind.

A search reaches inside a turn: a turn holding a row that says what you typed opens on that row for as long as the text is there, so typing crash opens the turns whose answers or calls say it. Taking the text back folds those turns again — a match is not a fold — while a turn you opened with → stays open through a search and through Tab, since that one is a fold you made. A turn whose question the search hides goes with it: what is left is the rows that say the word and the path down to each.

On a row that is not inside an open turn, ← folds the branch away: every row under it goes and the rows after it move up, leaving the branch as the one row it hangs from — on the line the conversation is on, that is the whole of the rest of the session. → on that row opens it again and gives back exactly what the fold took, including any fold made further down inside it. Neither key moves the highlight there. ← on a row with nothing under it on screen — a leaf, or a branch already folded — changes nothing, and neither does → on a row that is neither folded nor a turn.

A folded question can carry both folds, and → undoes the last one first: ← on it folds the rest of the session away below it, the first → gives that back, and the second opens its turn. Every fold lasts as long as the box is open and hides rows and nothing else: the session and the transcript are untouched, and the tree opens on every branch, with every turn folded, next time.

Home puts the highlight on the first row you can switch to — the turn under the session header, which is never a selection — and End on the last row of the tree, and each takes the box to that end. PageUp and PageDown move the box a window at a time and bring the highlight with them, so Enter never acts on a row that has scrolled out of sight.

Ctrl-F forks the highlighted row — on a folded turn, the last row of that turn, which is what Enter takes there too: the conversation from its start down to that row is copied into a session of its own, and you carry on in the copy. The box closes, the transcript shows the path down to that row, and the status line's session field carries the new id. What was said after that row, and every other branch, stays with the original — which is left exactly as it was, on the leaf it was on rather than on the row you forked, so going back to it is one /resume away with everything intact. It is /fork taken at the row you are looking at instead of at the leaf, and everything that command copies it copies: the subagent sessions that path ran, the scratch directory, and the labels naming rows on it.

A row you cannot switch to you cannot fork either: on the session header, and on anything below a node the session holds no event for, the key does nothing and the box stays open. Ctrl-F on the row the box opened on is /fork itself. Like /fork it is refused while a turn is running and while a subagent is still running, and unlike the Enter beside it, it interrupts nothing: the turn runs on, a line in the transcript says to wait for it, and nothing is copied. A live advisor is waited out rather than refused, on the same terms /fork keeps.

Ctrl-L names the highlighted row, and on a folded turn the question itself rather than the turn's end, because the row you are naming is the row you are reading. The box's title turns into a prompt, you type the name, and Enter saves it: the row then reads ▶ [fix-crash] user: Crash on startup, the label in square brackets between the mark and what the node is, in the color of whoever spoke. Esc leaves the prompt with nothing saved and the row as it was, and so does an Enter with nothing typed. The prompt owns every key while it is open: the letters go to the label, not to the search, and Tab and the arrows do nothing until it closes. Labelling a row again replaces its label.

A label is saved in the session, so it is there after a restart, and it belongs to the node rather than to the branch you were on: every branch that runs through that node shows it. It is an annotation and nothing more — it starts no branch, appears in no transcript, and is in no request to the model — so the conversation is exactly what it was before you wrote one. Tab reaches labels-only after no tools — three presses from all, since the cycle only goes forward — and it lists what you have named with the rows above each of them, opening the turn a labelled row sits in. Typing a label's text finds its row in any view that shows that row: the view and the search compose here as everywhere, so a labelled row the model sent is found by its label in all and in assistant-only, and one inside a turn opens that turn wherever it is found.

Session picker

Ctrl-S, or /resume, lists sessions created in this project directory. Tab widens to every session and back. Rows show the title, or the id for an untitled session, and carry up to two notes after it, in muted on every row: (clone) or (fork) on a session made by /clone or /fork, which is what tells a copy from the original it shares a title with, and (empty) on one holding no turn at all. A row that is both reads the title (clone) (empty): the badge says what the row is, the note after it what opening it would give you. The badge names the kind and not the id it was copied from — that is one /session away, and this is a list you scan. See Where a copy came from. The highlighted row reads as a row of the completion dock: a ❯ in the accent color at the box's left edge, and the name beside it in the accent too. Opening a session from another project is allowed; the workspace follows the session.

Settings

/settings covers the whole terminal with one box: the categories on the left, the selected category's rows on the right, two rows describing the highlighted row below them, and the keys you have across the bottom. ← and → move between the two lists, ↑ and ↓ move within the one that has the highlight and scroll it at the edges, the wheel scrolls whichever list the pointer is over, and PageUp and PageDown move the list holding the highlight by a window. Neither the wheel nor a page moves a highlight here, so scrolling previews no theme and replaces no category's rows; each list carries a scrollbar once it holds more rows than fit. Moving to another category puts the highlight on its first row. Esc closes it and gives back the transcript, the composer, and the status line exactly as they were.

The categories are Appearance and Model. Theme, the first row under Appearance, is the one row wired to a setting so far; the rest are placeholders that change nothing, and the settings behind them still live in the config files.

The Theme row shows the theme you are running. Enter on it replaces the rows with every theme you have, built in and your own, sorted by name with the one in force highlighted. ↑ and ↓ redraw the whole screen in the highlighted theme, so you see a theme before you keep it; Enter keeps it, and Esc puts back the theme you came in with and returns to the rows without saving anything.

Keeping a theme writes its name into your global config file as tui.theme.dark or tui.theme.light, whichever kind of terminal you are on, and it reaches the screen through the same once-a-second check that notices an edit you make in an editor — there is one path by which a theme takes effect, and the settings screen uses it. The write moves that one value and nothing else: your formatting, your key order, and keys Ashyo has never heard of all come back byte for byte. If the project's config file names a theme, that is the theme the row shows and the theme you keep seeing — the row says it comes from the project — and your choice still goes into your global file, in force wherever the project's is not. If the file cannot be written, the theme you chose stays for the rest of the session and the status line says why it was not saved.

Session

/session puts a box over the transcript with four things in it: this session's id and where it was copied from, the provider your next turn would run on, what the branch you are on holds, and what it has cost.

Origin is the row under the id, and it is there only on a session made by /clone or /fork: which kind of copy it was, and the id of the session it was copied from. A session you started rather than copied has no such row. The id is what ashyo session resume takes, so it is one command back to the source — and a copy of a copy names the copy it came from, so following the ids walks the chain back to the conversation it started from. See Where a copy came from.

Provider is the model the next turn would run on: the one the status line's model field shows, which is the /model pin on this branch or the first entry of the role. The provider reports its own API, credential type, and endpoint, so the box never guesses at them — both providers speak the OpenAI Responses API, openai-codex authenticates with an oauth credential and openrouter with an api_key, and the endpoint is the URL the request is posted to. A model reference no provider answers to is named on the two rows it can fill and stops there, and with no model configured at all both of them read none.

Messages counts the branch you are on, from the start of the session down to the leaf, across any /clear on it: a clear moves where the model starts reading and deletes nothing. User is the messages you typed, steers included; Assistant is the model's messages, one per provider pass; Tool Calls is the calls those messages asked for; Tool Results is the outputs sent back for them; Advisor is the advisor outputs put into the model's input. Total is the five added.

Tokens sums the same branch. Every answer records the usage its provider returned with it, in the session, so the numbers are there after a restart rather than starting again at zero. Input is the input tokens the provider did not serve from its cache and Cache Read is the ones it did, so the four rows add up and a cached turn reads as the cheap turn it was; Output counts reasoning tokens too; Total is the provider's own total. An answer recorded before Ashyo kept usage, or by a provider that returned none, counts as zero. The pass that writes a compaction summary is in here too — it ran on your model and spent your tokens — but it adds nothing to Messages, because nobody typed it and nothing answers it; a clean and a shake cost no request and appear nowhere. The advisor's tokens are not in here — they are the status line's advisorTokens field — and a subagent's session has its own.

The box is a snapshot of the moment it opened: a turn streaming behind it moves no number in it, and opening it again asks for the numbers again. It writes nothing to the transcript, records nothing in the session, and is not refused while a turn is running, since it changes nothing. A word after the command — /session x — is refused with a usage line rather than ignored.

Keys

F1 lists every binding. All four boxes scroll with the wheel, PageUp, and PageDown, and carry a scrollbar when their rows run past the box. Esc closes any overlay — in the session tree, the press after the one that clears its search — and the picker also closes with Ctrl-S however it was opened, this help with F1. A key an overlay does not bind does nothing at all: it closes nothing, and it does not reach the composer behind the box. The session tree binds every letter, which is its search.

Themes

Every color on the screen is one of eleven roles, and a theme is the one place they are given values. Two are built in: graphite for a terminal with a dark background and paper for one with a light background. Ashyo draws its first frame in the dark theme and asks the terminal what color its background is; a light answer moves it to the light theme. A terminal that does not answer — a multiplexer that swallows the query, for one — stays dark, and tui.theme.dark is then the theme in use on a light screen too.

Your own themes live in $XDG_CONFIG_HOME/ashyo/themes/, beside the global config file, one <name>.json per theme. The file name is the theme's name; there is no name field. A file named after a built-in replaces it. Name the one you want with tui.theme.dark and tui.theme.light in either config file, the project value winning over the global one, or pick one in the settings screen, which writes the global file for you.

A theme file has two objects. vars names colors you want to reuse; colors fills in the roles. A value is a #rrggbb triplet, the name of a var, or "" for the terminal's own color — nothing else, and a 256-color index in particular is an error: Ashyo names colors in 24 bits and lets the terminal's renderer lower them. A role you leave out keeps the value the built-in theme of the same kind gives it, so a short theme keeps working when a role is added. A key that is not a role is an error, because it is almost always a role misspelled.

~/.config/ashyo/themes/rust.json
{
  "vars": { "rust": "#d07020", "sand": "#e0c090" },
  "colors": {
    "text":         "",
    "muted":        "#a08060",
    "dim":          "#c09070",
    "accent":       "rust",
    "border":       "#804020",
    "borderAccent": "sand",
    "success":      "#70a040",
    "error":        "#e05050",
    "selectedBg":   "#402010",
    "user":         "#70b0e0",
    "assistant":    "#c090d0"
  }
}

mutedThe dock's unhighlighted descriptions, the session tree's lines and every row of it nobody spoke, the age column of the history dock and the four stat columns of the model picker — on every row of either, the highlighted one included — the picker's description row under its list, the argument hint drawn after the cursor, the track of every scrollbar, assistant and system turn headers.
dimThe status line, outside whatever its template puts in a color: wrapper.

Both config files and the file of the theme in use are checked once a second. Naming another theme, or saving the one you are running, recolors the screen within a second. A theme that cannot be used — missing, unparseable, holding a bad value or an unknown key — changes nothing: the theme in force stays and the status line names the theme file and the error until you fix it. That is also what happens at startup, where Ashyo runs in the built-in theme for the terminal's kind and shows the error; a broken theme never keeps Ashyo from starting.

Status line

The status line is yours to write. tui.statusLine is a template — literal text with placeholders in it — drawn once per frame and cut at the terminal's width with no mark. Write nothing and you get the default:

~/code/go/ashyo · GPT 5.6 Luna · xhigh · advisor ready                                            237.4k / 1m █████▋░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░

which is this template:

%{color:user %{projectDir}} %{color:muted %{separator}} %{color:accent %{modelName}}%{if:effort  %{color:muted %{separator}} %{effort}}%{if:advisor  %{color:muted %{separator}} advisor %{advisor}}%{if:mode  %{color:muted %{separator}} %{mode}}%{right}%{contextTokens} / %{contextMaxTokens} %{contextProgress}

Reading across: the project directory, in the theme's user blue; the model as the catalog names it, in the accent color; the reasoning effort your next request carries; the advisor's state, when you have one; and plan when you are planning — each after a muted tui.statusSeparator. Then, against the right edge: what the model read on its last answer — on a long turn, on its latest block — against the window it read into, and the same as a bar. With no advisor and no plan the left part ends at the model.

The template lives in either config file, the project's overriding your global one, and it is applied live like every other tui key: save the file and the next frame is drawn from it. A template that does not parse is a configuration error like any other — the line you were seeing stays, and the error names tui.statusLine, the column, and what is wrong with it.

Syntax

WrittenDraws
%{name}One field: %{model} draws gpt-5.6-luna.
%{color:role text}text in one of the theme's foreground roles — text, muted, dim, accent, border, borderAccent, success, error, user, assistant. It may hold fields and further wrappers: %{color:accent %{modelName}}.
%{if:name text}text, but only while that field has something behind it. It is what lets a line say nothing about a thing that is not there: %{if:plan plan %{plan}} draws nothing until you are planning, and the default's %{if:effort …} draws nothing on a model with no reasoning control. Which fields can be empty is in the table below.
%{right}Everything after it against the terminal's right edge, with the cells between blank. Once per template, and not inside a wrapper. A bar with no width of its own on the right stretches back toward the left part, so the blank is what the bar's cap leaves.
%%One %. Any other % is itself, so 24% needs no escape and %{ is written %%{.

Whitespace is yours: nothing is trimmed and nothing is collapsed. The one space between a wrapper's name and its text is the separator; every space after it is drawn. Outside every wrapper the line is drawn in the theme's dim role, as the whole line always was.

Fields

A count is drawn to one decimal with k, or in millions with m, a trailing zero dropped, and plain below a thousand: 812, 237.4k, 1m, 1.05m. The two counts that move as you work, contextTokens and advisorTokens, are right-aligned in six cells —  12.3k, 237.4k,  1.05m — so the unit letter and whatever follows it stay in one column while the count grows; a window is drawn as it is. A percentage is whole from ten up and to one decimal below it: 24%, 5.1%, 0.4%. A field with nothing behind it draws nothing at all, and if is how you step around it: effort on a model with no reasoning control, effortChosen whenever the model can be asked for the level you chose, plan and mode outside plan mode, advisor and its counts when you have no advisor, and every count, percentage, and bar until there is a window to measure against. Once there is one, a conversation nothing has been read into yet reads 0, 0.0%, and an empty bar, so the line has its shape before the first answer and keeps it. The rest carry a word for as long as a session is open — memory is on or off — so an if around one of those always draws.

The context bar

%{contextProgress} draws contextPercent as a bar that fills by eighths of a cell: █ for each full cell, then one of ▏▎▍▌▋▊▉ for the remainder, then a muted rail of ░. The fill is green below half the window, the accent color from half to 80%, and red from 80% on, in the theme's success, accent, and error roles, so your theme decides the actual colors. Nothing is written into the bar; put %{contextPercent} beside it if you want the number.

The bar takes whatever width the terminal has left after everything else on the line, up to twenty-four cells, and shrinks below that to whatever is left — down to one cell, and to none when nothing is left — so on a narrow terminal, or a line with a lot on it, the bar gives way before anything else does. %{contextProgress:24} fixes it at that many cells instead; a line holding two bars gives each half of what is left.

What a template cannot leave out

When a config file, or the file of the theme in use, fails to parse, the file and the error are appended to the line in the error role whatever your template says — a template is one of the things that can be broken, so it cannot hide the message saying so. While you are typing a transcript search, the search prompt replaces the line.
